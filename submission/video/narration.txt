Hex Machina is a cooperative spell debugger. Its executable graph is shared by a person and their browser agent.

This spell should water a rare Moonflower. Instead, Multiply runs before rain has a bounded target. Twelve lunar ducks flood the observatory.

Moth suggests suspicious runes, while WebMCP tools inspect exact graph state and trace the responsible path. The deterministic simulator remains authoritative.

Then the person contributes something the graph cannot know: taste. The ducks are funny, so they become a sacred constraint. Deleting them is no longer valid.

The agent searches under that constraint and proposes umbrellas, redirecting rain onto the flower. The versioned diff changes nothing until the person approves.

After approval, the application validates and applies the patch atomically. Umbrella and Bloom activate; Pour targets Moonflower. A new cast returns Stable, with the room dry and the sacred ducks preserved.

Hex Machina shows why graph interfaces matter: humans and agents negotiate executable intent instead of guessing from pixels. Magic is just code with worse documentation.
